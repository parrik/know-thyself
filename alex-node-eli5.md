# Alex graph — plain-English overlays

ELI5 overlays for the Alex case study. Each overlay sits ABOVE the original
node statement in the dashboard drawer. The original statement is preserved
unchanged for citation; the overlay exists to help a bounded reader land
quickly.

Voice: editorial, conversational, lightly direct. The way Alex would
explain her own graph to someone who hasn't worked in publishing.

---

## Schema words

### Reference (R)
A biographical fact. Single source, in principle verifiable. The kind of
thing that goes on a back-cover bio or in a case file: where Alex was
born, where she went to school, what her sister's name was. References
are the load-bearing baseline; every richer claim eventually traces back
to one.

### Observation (O)
A specific episode with a date attached. Not a pattern, not a theory —
the actual evening of October 19 when Alex realized she hadn't heard the
first half of Mira's sentence. Observations are the raw material.
Patterns get built from them; if the observation is wrong, everything
downstream goes wrong with it.

### Overlap (P)
A pattern that shows up across two or more independent episodes. Not
"she did this once" but "this shape keeps recurring." The threshold is
real: a single instance is a novel; only when a second independent case
lands does it earn the P-prefix. Overlaps are where the graph starts
to predict instead of just describe.

### Novel (N)
A tentative interpretation built on a single derivation. The PROPOSED
prefix and the mandatory caveats block are not decoration — they're how
Alex marks "this might be wrong" to her future self. Novels age into
overlaps when corroborated, or quietly fall when they don't. Most do
the second.

### Emergent (E)
A claim that exists only at the intersection of two or more parent
nodes — present in neither alone. The most interesting and most
fragile node type. If a parent gets revised, the emergent has to be
re-derived from scratch, not patched.

### Practice (PR)
An operating rule Alex actually lives by, derived from a descriptive
claim. Not a goal, not an aspiration — a commitment with a calendar
slot. Every practice in this graph traces back to an overlap or
emergent that earned it. Floating rules go in goals, not here.

### Open question (OQ)
An unresolved question, held first-class so it doesn't get quietly
absorbed into a confident answer. The function is structural:
some questions deserve to stay open for years. The graph protects
them from premature closure.

### Equivalency (EQ)
A bridge to an external theoretical framework. Names that what Alex
is doing has a formal analog somewhere — useful as orientation, but
the equivalency does not reduce her case to the framework. It points,
it does not collapse.

## Practice menu

### Sunday 8–10am on the Lakefront Trail is protected
The non-negotiable that holds the rest up. Alex meets the running group
at the 57th Street beach house at 8am, runs the lakefront, doesn't take
work calls or family scheduling in the slot. When travel breaks the
Sunday, she moves it to Saturday — never skips. Mira's stability indexes
off this run, so it is parenting infrastructure, not a hobby.

### Read every submitted manuscript in one sitting, pen in hand
Inherited from her FSG mentor Drenka Willen. Phone off, dining table
cleared, three to five hours, marginal notes in pencil and structural
notes in the blue-ruled notebook afterward. The whole has a shape a
partial read cannot see. Holds Saturday mornings in commissioning
season, no exceptions.

### Reveal thyself — monthly commitment review
The last Sunday of each month, thirty minutes at the kitchen table.
List every active commitment — manuscripts, dinners, letters,
committee seats, side reads — and ask of each: does it serve the
spine, or is it a beautiful sentence I'm refusing to cut? Kill at
least one thing. This is "kill your darlings" lifted out of editing
and applied to the life.

### No email Saturday before 12pm
PR02 at finer granularity. Six in the morning to noon, the phone goes
in the kitchen drawer and the manuscript comes out. When this breaks
during the fall ramp, Alex treats the break itself as a canary —
the slip of the rule is information.

### One long walk per month without a phone
A two- to three-hour walk alone, no phone, no manuscript, usually
Hyde Park to Jackson Park with a coffee at the Point. Not editorial —
the explicit point is to be in a mode that is not interpretive. A
direct hedge against P03. Half the time the walk produces nothing,
and that counts as the practice working.

### Annual 3-day retreat alone (first May 2025, Door County)
Three days once a year. Mira with Daniel or with grandparents, no
authors, no manuscripts, no calls. A rented cabin near Ephraim. The
output is a Moleskine notebook and nothing else — no resolutions, no
report-outs, nothing deliverable. KT01 at the annual granularity.

### Know-thyself — edit the self as a manuscript
The meta-practice. Twice a year on a long lakefront walk, read the
recent months the way you would read a submission: what is the spine?
What is load-bearing? What is a beautiful sentence that doesn't serve?
Where am I treating a novel as if it were an overlap? This graph and
the act of maintaining it are the instrument.

## Nodes

### NOW
Read this first. Nine months into Chicago, the routine is back, Mira
is stable, the Chen book is signed, and the question is whether any
of this holds up or whether it depends on conditions Alex hasn't
named. The week, the month, and the quarter each have their own short
list. Standing rules at the bottom; canaries to watch for at the
very bottom.

### R01-origin
Born 1980 in Rhinebeck, raised over a bookshop her mother ran on East
Market Street until 2008. Father taught high-school English. Evenings
were reading aloud; the back room of the shop was where homework got
done. The household register — books as the center of gravity — never
left her, and the shop's eventual closing still shapes how she thinks
about money.

### R02-career
Started at Farrar, Straus and Giroux in 2005 under Jonathan Galassi;
moved to Oxford University Press in 2009; senior editor by 2019,
commissioning eight books a year in philosophy and religious studies.
Known at OUP for taking first books from junior scholars when the
prose was good and for editing line by line in an era when most
acquisitions editors had stopped.

### R03-family
Divorced in 2019 after eleven years. Daniel went on doing finance in
Brooklyn. Mira (born 2011) has lived with Alex more than ninety
percent of the time since — formally shared custody, practically not.
Daniel sees Mira about one weekend a month and over school breaks.
The handoff conversation happens by email.

### R04-move
August 2024: sold the Prospect Heights apartment, bought a three-
bedroom greystone in Hyde Park, enrolled Mira at Kenwood Academy,
took the director-level role at the University of Chicago Press.
Six weeks from offer to closing. Mira was consulted but not
consenting in any meaningful sense. The pay bump and the lower cost
of living added up to a real income jump; the rest of the math
(see R23) was less tidy than planned.

### R05-sister
Helen, three years older, killed at twenty-three in a single-car
accident on Route 9G outside Rhinebeck in September 2007. BAC over
the limit. What Alex won't say out loud, but the graph records:
she rarely brings Helen up directly, and when she does it's "my
sister used to say" rather than the register of grief. Other nodes
lean on this one without naming it.

### R06-substances
Heavy smoker through her late twenties, quit cold after a pneumonia
episode in 2013. Drinking sat at three to five a week through the
marriage, climbed post-divorce, spiked to twelve to fourteen a week
in the first three Chicago months, back to about six a week by April.
Alex tracks this herself in a notebook. She has not raised it with
her therapist.

### R12-canary-missed-run
Two Sundays in a row missed without a Saturday replacement and the
routine is breaking. Act on it before the drinking number rises —
by the time R13 trips you're already downstream. The earliest
operational signal Alex has from the body side.

### R13-canary-drinking-over-eight
Baseline post-move is six a week. Two consecutive weeks above eight
and the Sep–Nov 2024 shape is coming back. Probably the shortest-
latency signal Alex has for isolation creep — see N01.

### R14-canary-silent-mornings
Mira talks at breakfast. That is the default state. When the
mornings go quiet for a full week, something downstream of E01 is
deteriorating, usually before grades show it. Cheap signal, high
information.

### R15-canary-political-budget
Alex holds her ground at the Press; that has costs (see P02). A
second Ferraro-type committee fight in a single quarter, before the
first one publishes, means the political budget is overdrawn —
P02 is becoming a liability rather than an asset.

### R37-canary-crossword-stops
The Saturday crossword has not missed a Saturday since 2014 outside
of hospital or funeral. Two consecutive misses without one of those
causes and something is deteriorating — often quieter and earlier
than the routine-breakdown signals, because the crossword is
effortless and gets dropped before the effortful rituals do.

### R38-canary-nadia-uncalled
Nadia leaves a voicemail every three weeks or so. Baseline callback
latency is one to three days. Voicemail that sits a full week is the
drift becoming permanent rather than logistic. Less a week-of-work
signal than a quarter-of-life signal.

### R39-canary-old-contracts
When Alex is escalation-of-commitment-ing toward the Brooklyn life
she left, the giveaway is not talking about Brooklyn — it is
spending a Saturday morning re-reading the contract archive from a
2015 OUP book. Twice since the move. A third instance in a single
quarter and OQ01 is drifting toward permanent-stay by default rather
than by decision.

### R07-lens-kill-darlings
"Murder your darlings" — Quiller-Couch via Perkins. The discipline
of cutting a beautiful sentence when it doesn't serve the whole. Alex
uses it editorially as first principle, then turns it on her own
calendar through PR03.

### R08-lens-iceberg
Hemingway's claim that seven-eighths of a story is underwater and
omission strengthens what remains. Alex applies it to author bios
and to her own self-report — both, arguably, to a fault. Half of
P03 is iceberg theory misapplied to a self.

### R09-lens-close-reading
Read the sentence, not around it. The Richards / Empson / Brooks
tradition: the text is self-sufficient and rewards attention at
word-and-phrase grain. The basis of how Alex edits and the spine
of the one-sitting read.

### R11-lens-structural-vs-line
Three levels of editing with three different interventions:
structural (does the argument work?), line (does each sentence do
its job?), copy (is it correct?). Alex's role is structural by
contract; the line work is hers by habit, not assignment.

### R36-navarro-treatment
The signature: the first thirty pages of a manuscript come back
with dense blue-pencil marginalia, then a four-to-eight-page
structural memo instead of line edits past page thirty. The signal
to the author is that the structural problem must be solved before
line work matters. Authors call it the hardest week of their
professional lives. Alex declines invitations to write about it.

### O01-first-three-months
September through November 2024. The hardest stretch since Helen
died. Fifty-hour weeks ramping on Rachel Liebowitz's inherited
list, two author dinners a week, Mira's grades dropping from A/B
to C/D, no friends in Chicago, running stopped, sleep under six
hours, drinking up to twelve to fourteen a week. The October 19
moment — Mira asked her something at dinner and Alex realized she
had not heard the first half of the sentence — is the one she
keeps coming back to.

### O02-running-group
December 8, 2024. Joined the Chicago Lakefront Sunday Runners — 8am
at the 57th Street beach house, eight-mile loop. First weeks felt
forced. By week four, Priya Menon (radiologist at UChicago Medicine)
and Marcus Okafor (architect) were the regular conversation. The
routine restarted around the social anchor, not around willpower —
and when Priya travels, Alex's adherence drops the week after. That
detail is what made N01 surfaceable.

### O03-therapist-referral
February 2025. Priya referred her to Dr. Ana Rivera, a cognitive-
analytic therapist in Hyde Park. Weekly fifty-minute sessions, $180
out of pocket once the UChicago plan's twelve sessions ran out.
Alex's framing as of May: "mostly noticing patterns in how I react,
not unpacking history." What she has not raised: Helen, the affair,
the drinking uptick. Private about session content; do not push.

### O04-daughter-grades-recovered
April 2025 report card: back to A/B across five subjects. Alex
attributes the recovery to Ms. Okonkwo's after-school writing group
and to the household stabilizing as her own routine came back.
Mira, in passing, said "it's easier when you're not weird in the
mornings" — a sentence the graph has been rotating ever since.

### O05-workplace-conflict
March 11, 2025. David Ferraro (philosophy, twenty-two years at the
Press) opposed Alex's proposal to commission a first book from
Amara Chen — assistant professor at Northwestern, philosophy of
climate modeling. Alex held the position, brought the rest of the
committee around over two weeks, contract out April 3. David has
been formal-polite since. Alex has not initiated repair and
probably won't.

### O06-post-divorce-affair
March 2020 to July 2021. Eighteen months with a philosopher she
was editing, started right after the divorce, ended by email when
she got concerned about professional entanglement. The author's
book came out at OUP through a different editor in 2022. Alex
has told no one at UChicago and has not raised it with her
therapist. Load-bearing for N02 and P03; do not raise unprompted.

### O07-kitchen-dinners
Once a Friday a month since January. Four to six UChicago authors
and series editors at the Hyde Park house. Alex cooks Lebanese-
inflected from her mother's side, pours generously, keeps the
conversation going until midnight. Several authors have said the
best editorial conversation of their book happened at her table.
Mira eats upstairs.

### O08-correspondence-discipline
Since around 2010, Alex has kept handwritten paper letters going
with three authors she edited early at OUP — a poet in Iowa, a
philosopher in Edinburgh, a historian in Cape Town. About six
letters a year each. The circle has been closed since 2014. When
everything else slips, this doesn't.

### O11-passed-on-prizewinner
April 2016. Alex passed on a proposal from a first-time historian.
A competing press took it; the book was shortlisted for a major
prize in 2020 and won a subsidiary one. Her reasoning was
defensible at the time; the published book did substantially fix
the problem she had flagged. She still thinks about it — not as
"I was wrong" but as "I was right in a way that would have been
wrong to act on for much longer."

### O12-inherited-author-third-book
Inherited a continental philosopher when Rachel retired in 2018.
Edited his second book successfully in 2019. His third arrived in
2022 with a structural problem Alex flagged immediately; two years
of revision could not resolve it without dismantling the book's
premise. Published 2024 to polite-weak reviews. The author still
writes her warmly. She reads the reviews as her failure.

### O13-reputation-for-no
Alex is known at academic conferences for the fast, clear no when
the fit is wrong. Agents call it an asset. Junior colleagues call
it brittle — the speed of "no" sometimes closes a door that
deserved a second conversation. Both are true. Alex tracks this and
hasn't changed the practice.

### P01-routine-as-regulation
When Alex's running and sleep break, other things deteriorate
proportionally — work hours stretch, responsiveness to Mira drops,
drinking rises, mood lowers. When the routine returns, these
stabilize. Three independent episodes; the drinking line gives a
fourth corroborating axis. Not preference — appears structural.

### P02-holds-positions-in-work
In editorial contexts, Alex holds a minority position and persuades
toward it rather than yielding for social ease. The Chen contract
is the current win; the cooled relationship with Ferraro is the
current cost. Two earlier OUP stretches (2016, 2021) fit the same
shape. The political budget for this is finite.

### P03-interpretive-depth-emotional-distance
Going extremely deep on interpretation — of manuscripts, of
situations — while keeping a specific kind of distance from
feeling. Visible across the unrepaired Ferraro conflict, the
end-by-email of the 2020–2021 affair, the framing of therapy as
pattern-finding rather than processing, and what she hasn't raised
with her therapist. Not denial — closer to a policy. Generative
for editing; costly where someone needs her present feeling-first.

### P04-ruthless-editorial-time
Editorial calendar is tight, meetings run to the minute, the
fast-no, the structural-memo-not-line-edits, the closed letter
circle, the once-a-month batched dinners. Editorial time is a
budget she runs a hard ledger on. When the soft accommodations
leak in (answering Yonas late but generously), the mechanism is
editorial care, not calendar slack. Sits in productive contradiction
with P05.

### P05-overbooks-socially
Since the move, the calendar is socially overbooked in a way the
Brooklyn calendar never was — kitchen dinners, Sunday runs and
weekday coffees, three committee memberships she didn't need to
accept, two series-advisory roles. The density exceeds stated need.
Both this and P04 are simultaneously true: the calendar is efficient
AND overbooked, because the overbooking sits inside socially
legitimate work cover. Don't reconcile; both are the content.

### P06-holds-grudges
A single shape across multiple domains. Rachel's retirement
announcement was polite, unwarm — there's a 2020 committee vote
Alex has never mentioned but remembers. Ferraro: no repair. The
2020 affair ended by email and framed as professional caution. The
2016 prizewinner pass-on revisited with a particular edge. The
specific tell: when she says "I'm fine," a small precise story about
the other person's error follows three sentences later.

### P07-care-real-and-substitute
The kitchen dinners, the fifteen-year letters, the Navarro treatment,
the $180/session out-of-pocket therapy, the willingness to lose
Ferraro over Chen — the care is real, specific, costly, chosen. It
is also the socially sanctioned channel for the attachment energy
Alex doesn't channel elsewhere. Both claims hold simultaneously. The
graph isn't trying to resolve which dominates; the claim is that the
channel is doing both kinds of work at once.

### N01-isolation-is-early-warning
PROPOSED: long stretches without meaningful social contact aren't
neutral for Alex — they appear upstream of routine breakdown. Sep–Nov
2024 is the clearest case; the running group restart suggests a
social anchor (not willpower) was what let the routine return. Could
be wrong: O01 conflated several variables, only one detailed
episode so far, and isolation might be coincident rather than
causal. Needs a second independent case.

### N02-therapy-as-pattern-finding
PROPOSED: Alex's framing of therapy as "noticing patterns" suggests
she's using it primarily as cognitive self-observation rather than
as emotional processing of specific events. May be appropriate for
stage; may also be another instance of P03 — going interpretive
instead of going toward the feeling. Speculative; rests on one
short sentence Alex offered.

### N03-helen-structural-presence
PROPOSED: Alex's willingness to take first books from women writers
under thirty-five — more pronounced than her peers — has a plausible
ground in the Helen loss she has not surfaced. Helen was twenty-three
when she died and had been halfway through a novel no one ever read.
The pattern is real; the proposed explanation is novel and could be
wrong in either direction. Should probably stay novel indefinitely
unless Alex names the connection.

### N04-risk-corridor
TENTATIVE. Five low-probability, high-consequence scenarios where
the graph would invert its current trajectory: a Mira crisis forcing
a return East; a UChicago leadership change removing autonomy; the
2020 affair surfacing publicly; the drinking crossing a visible line;
a Helen-anniversary compounding event. Not a prediction — a benchmark
against which surprise becomes visible. Each scenario has a named
canary and, where possible, a counter-move.

### N05-move-was-grief-shaped
PROPOSED: the Helen loss in 2007, the twenty years in which the loss
was never directly processed, and the August 2024 move — done in a
six-week window, at the symbolic age of forty-four, into a city
where Alex knew no one — are plausibly a single shape rather than
three independent events. The move accomplishes geographic distance
from the Brooklyn years where the loss sat unaddressed. The
opportunity framing is real and the grief shape may be concurrent;
not mutually exclusive. Highly speculative; do not propagate without
Alex naming it.

### N06-mira-structurally-parental
PROPOSED: specific behaviors in Mira since the Sep–Nov stretch
suggest a low-grade role-inversion. The sidelong comment about "the
wine schedule." Covering the Kenwood morning check-in on days Alex
overslept. Asking peer-register questions like "how did the Ferraro
thing resolve?" Mira's "easier when you're not weird in the mornings"
reads in this frame as a parental sentence. Could be normal
adolescent perceptiveness; the worry is shape, not quantity. Do not
raise with Mira unprompted.

### E01-child-stability-depends-on-alex-stability
Emerges from P01 (routine-as-regulation) crossed with O04 (Mira's
recovery coinciding with Alex's routine returning). Neither node
alone produces the claim — P01 is about Alex, O04 is about Mira.
Together: protecting Alex's run is parenting infrastructure, not
self-indulgence. Mira's own sentence corroborates but isn't the
basis.

### E02-hospitality-as-single-non-solitary-ritual
Emerges from O07 (the kitchen dinners) crossed with P03 (interpretive
depth, emotional distance). P03 alone says Alex keeps distance; O07
alone describes a social practice. Together: the dinners are the
only sustained site where her interpretive mode and her presence to
others are co-located, because the editor/author frame permits depth
without exposure. The letter correspondence is the non-social twin.
Operationally: the dinners are not optional, they are structural.

### E03-forecast-1-month
By May 31: Chen edit delivered; Sunday runs held; drinking at or
below six a week; Dr. Rivera session count at sixteen with either
Helen or the drinking raised (not both, per the NOW coin-flip);
Ferraro thaw will not happen because P03 won't initiate. OQ01 and
OQ02 still unanswered. High confidence — short horizon, calendar-
bound variables, low chance Alex surprises herself.

### E04-forecast-90-day
By July 31: Chen book into external review; second commissioning
target either secured or openly failing; the Priya-Marcus weekday-
dinner friendship will solidify or it won't (three months of
Sunday-only is the variable). Mira's summer resolves OQ02 by force
one way or the other. OQ01 still open but the August 2026 two-year
anniversary starts to look close enough to require a decision by
fall. Medium variance; the hinges are named.

### E05-forecast-12-month
By April 2026: OQ01 decided explicitly because Mira's sophomore
year and the 2027 commissioning slate force the question. Chen book
publishes September 2025 and the acknowledgements will tell OQ03
one way or the other. N01 either gets a second corroborating case
or quietly drops. N03 stays novel — Alex doesn't voluntarily name
structural-ground claims about Helen. At least one new overlap node
nobody currently sees. High variance. Function: a benchmark for
surprise, not a prediction.

### E06-intimacy-mediated-by-editorial-frame
Emerges from the cluster: kitchen dinners, the closed letter
correspondence, the no-contemporary-fiction-while-editing rule, the
Navarro treatment, the affair being with an author, the dating-app
pattern of short things ended by text. No single node holds the
claim. The editorial frame is not just where Alex works — it is the
frame through which intimacy itself is legible to her. Reframes
OQ04: the question isn't "does she want a partner," it is "can she
build an intimacy outside the editorial frame."

### E07-helen-shapes-commissioning
Emerges from N03 (Helen-structural) intersecting N05 (move was
grief-shaped). If both novels hold, the editorial signature of
first-books-from-women-under-thirty-five isn't a preference — it
is a structural response to Helen's half-finished novel. The Chen
acquisition specifically — a thirty-four-year-old first-book author
Alex fought the committee for — sits at the intersection. Doubly
tentative because it rests on two novels; if either falls, this
falls.

### E08-forecast-5-year
By May 2030: Mira is nineteen and probably in college on the East
Coast (aging father, Nadia in Boston, residual Brooklyn weight); if
she's in Chicago, OQ01 has resolved toward permanent. Alex is
forty-nine with perimenopause fully landed. Father has either
declined significantly or died. OQ04 either resolved or still
unnamed. At UChicago: either Alex is Press director or she has
moved. At least one structural feature of this graph has been wrong
enough to require revision. The point is the surprise-benchmark; if
this all comes true, the graph has gone stale.

### EQ01-scaffolding-through-ritual
P01 has a close analog in the contemplative literature on practice
and ritual — repeated physical practice maintaining the internal
conditions for clarity. Alex grew up reading the religious-studies
shelf in her mother's bookshop; she has vocabulary for this. The
equivalency names a thing without reducing P01 to the external
frame.

### OQ01-long-term-plan
Is Chicago a two-to-three-year plan or a permanent relocation? Alex
has not decided. The decision affects Mira's college-application
strategy, the shape of the UChicago list past a two-year horizon,
and whether to actively maintain the Brooklyn professional network.
Do not let this question get quietly absorbed into an assumed
answer.

### OQ02-relationship-with-ex
Is the distance from Daniel stable or in drift? Mira has seen him
twice since the move — Thanksgiving and spring break — less than
formal custody anticipates. Practical constraint (eight hundred
miles) or slow drift Alex has feelings about but hasn't named?
Touches Mira's college choices, Alex's relationship with her own
parents, and how permanent OQ01 ends up being.

### OQ03-sustainability-of-distance
Is P03 sustainable in the role? Senior editorship increasingly asks
the editor to function like an emotional confidant for authors —
particularly junior scholars under tenure pressure. Alex's
characteristic distance may be a feature for the editorial work
itself and a liability in the relational half of the role. Open in
both directions: should she adjust, or should she stay, recognizing
that the distance is part of what makes the editing work.

### OQ04-partner-or-enough
Long-term partner, or is the editorial and authorial intimacy
enough? Alex has not named this question to herself as a question.
She has given it tactical answers (the app, the three brief things)
and structural answers (the dinners, the letters). E06 reframes
it: the live question is capacity for intimacy outside the
editorial frame. The right posture may be to leave it open another
five years and see what precipitates.

### OQ05-press-director-succession
What does Alex's UChicago list look like if the current director
retires in two to three years? Alex is a possible internal
candidate. A new director with a different list shape — someone
less committed to the structural-memo register — would change the
environment around her hold-positions pattern. If Alex is the
successor, OQ01 resolves toward permanent by force. Two different
institutional futures sit under this question. She hasn't surfaced
it; she will.
